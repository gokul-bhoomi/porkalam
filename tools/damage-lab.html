Arena damage lab

The floor is sand on a river island. Real sand doesn't crack like a plate: it gets pushed, thrown, burnt, wet and fused. Each mark below follows that idea.

Ideas

  1. Craters, not cracks. A heavy hit pushes the sand into a bowl with a raised rim, shadow on the far wall and light on the near wall, matching the sunset behind. Thrown sand lands around it in grains and paler streaks.
  2. Skid grooves. When a fighter is knocked back, their feet plough two grooves and leave a heap of sand where they stop. The arena shows where the big hits came from.
  3. Footprints. Fighters leave prints wherever they walk. By the end, the middle of the island is trampled and the edges are clean.
  4. Element marks. Fire leaves a black burn with embers that cool from orange to grey and a little smoke. Lightning fuses sand into black glass branches (fulgurite) that glow blue, then orange, then go dark. River splashes darken the sand, and it slowly dries from the edges.
  5. Power impacts. A deep crater, long ray streaks, clods of wet sand thrown out, ripples frozen in the sand, and river water seeping up into the bottom of the crater, reflecting the sky.
  6. Damage that stays. The game keeps only the newest 16 cracks, fading over 30 s. The marks could be drawn once onto a hidden floor layer, so a whole match of damage stays with no slowdown. Only the moving parts (embers, smoke, drying) are redrawn each frame.
  7. Optional painted marks. The examples here are drawn in code. For the most real look, ChatGPT could paint a sheet of top-down sand marks (crater, burn, glass) matching the arena art, with code still doing the motion, as the divine weapons do.

How they'd map to the game

Hit damage sets the crater size, knockback sets the skid, fire/lightning/water moves pick their element, and power moves get the big impact. Light hits leave only a scuff and a few grains, so the floor isn't covered in seconds.
